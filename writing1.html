1. What is a ritual, practice, or routine in your life that is important for your psychological wellbeing and/or fulfillment? Why?

How does this ritual or behavior contribute to your life? What aspect of it makes it valuable to you? What can it teach us about the nature or ingredients of human fulfillment more broadly?


My most important ritual is to walk from my architecture studio back to my dorm on foot, and to have a 30mins snack time no matter when of the day. This routine helps clear up my mind from all the repetitive iterating moments in my design processes and prepares me for bed time. Normally this will be 2am or 3am late night, and neither school shuttles nor golf cars are running. Under this circumstance, my friend prefers to call an uber considering the late night cold and the safety concerns. This shortens the trip back to dorm from 15 mins to 5 mins and completely cut off my thinking process, which is a transitional moment that helps me recognize my own progress each day.

I think human fulfillment largely consists of self-achievement and a certain satisfaction from all the rituals we will carry on everyday (like sleeping, eating, exercising). My specific ritual is like a “liminal space” in between my work everyday as a designer and my ordinary life as a human.



2. What is a human-made creation that brings out the best in you? Why?

This topic can go back to prosthetic-like technologies/artifacts.

For me the best one is definitely my contact lens. I begin having nearsightedness since my elementary school and now I have a -3.75 for my left eye and a -4.25 for my right eye. I’ve been always told by my mom that I look nerdy and ugly in my glasses. She always told me that I have beautiful eyes (probably because I got that from her) and just don’t want the glasses to “block” them. The stronger my prescription, the more pronounced the visual magnification effect of this concave lens becomes. So, I persisted in only wearing glasses when I can’t read the words on the blackboard in elementary school. To some extent this worsen my prescription by a lot. When I got into high school I immediately got off my glasses and put on contacts instead. The same time I got off all of my mom’s complaints about glasses.

On the positive side, I do appreciate contacts right now. I do feel that wearing contacts rather than glasses improves my appearance a lot, and in turn it boost my confidence. I also am not a huge fan of having something attached to my face. Glasses will also become foggy whenever I have hot food, which is super annoying.



3. When do you cherish the slow or hard way of doing something? Why?When do you embrace constraints or prefer inefficiency? When do you willingly sacrifice convenience or comfort? What does the limited, slower, or more effortful approach offer that the quicker and easier way does not? What can we learn from this about human fulfillment and the kinds of technologies that might support it?

The most recent example is my wired headphones. I switched from AirPods to this because I accidentally put my AirPods into laundry one day. The wired headphones are simply an older version of AirPods and they function basically the same way. But the way wired headphones attached to my phone is different. While AirPods are completely detached, wired headphones are almost like an additional accessory to my phone. I don’t necessarily have to unplug them when I’m not wearing them, so there’s not such a risk of losing my headphones somewhere. The two buds are physically connected to each other through the wire, which also solves the very common problem of losing one bud (which also happened to me once).

More than that, the wires of headphones gives me a sense of wearing vintage music players (walkman, etc.), which matches with the fashion trends nowadays. They also give hint to other people on “this person is listening to music, so they might not wanna be disturbed”. I do sacrifice the beautiful quality and stereo sound that comes with AirPods, but the convenience of wired headphones wins over them. Human fulfillment can be understood as some sort of push of technology advancement, but improvement doesn’t always bring satisfying outcomes. There’s some very special qualities about vintage products.



4. What is something you appreciate or long for from the past? Why?

It could be something you lived through, or something from the distant past. It might be a tradition or an antiquated value that you appreciate, an old way of doing things that you prefer / are fond of, or even a personal time or cultural moment that you hope to reconnect with. What do you appreciate about it? What wisdom does it contain? What can it teach us about living well today?

I especially appreciate a moment of silence and boredom, something I haven’t been having for a long time. Before phone, computer and everything, me in elementary school always indulged in my own imagination. When I didn’t want to do homework, I always stare outside of the window or straight to the pencil case in front of me. I would observe their textures, colors, the roundness of the pencil case’s edges, and I would always play around with the arrangement of things. Beyond that, I could also go to my imaginative world, where my friends and I were comic artists. We started from making four-panel comedy strip and actually sold those to our classmates 2 cents per copy. To complete the setting of this world, I started to draw sections of buildings where the whole comic-making process happened. I even made an amusement park design for me and my bestie.The reason I miss this is because after I got my electronic devices, I scroll on my phone to spend some spare times. My imagination and creativity isn’t that vivid as before. Maybe that just happens as I grow.
